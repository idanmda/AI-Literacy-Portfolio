About the class

A practical introduction to using AI creatively, responsibly, and critically.

MDA AI Literacy

In MDA AI Literacy, we explore different forms of artificial intelligence by creating real projects instead of only learning the theory. The class includes a collaboration with Vjal Institute and gives us experience with generative AI tools for images, music, customized assistants, audio, and presentations.

Across the projects, I learned that AI works best when the user gives clear instructions, checks important claims, and improves the first result instead of accepting it automatically. I also learned that different tools are useful for different types of work.

Production 03 · AI Agent

Homework Planner

I built a customized Gemini Gem for high school students who have several assignments and need help deciding what to do first. It prioritizes tasks using due dates, estimated time, and difficulty.

Creation processI gave the agent specific rules, tested it with normal, incomplete, and limitation-focused prompts, and then improved it by telling it to keep answers short and focused on planning.
Real-life useA planning assistant like this can help students organize homework, create realistic schedules, ask for missing information, and reduce the stress of deciding where to start.
Testing highlights
Normal test
Correctly prioritized the assignment due first and organized the rest of the week.
Difficult test
Asked for due dates, time, and difficulty when the prompt did not include enough information.
Limitation test
Did not complete an entire essay; instead, it redirected the user toward planning and organization.

Main limitation: the quality of the plan depends on the accuracy of the information the student provides.

Production 05 · AI Presentation

How the 3-Point Shot Changed Basketball

I created a four-slide presentation explaining why the 3-point shot became so important in modern basketball. The main ideas were how it changed scoring and how shooters create more space for the offense.

Creation processI used ChatGPT to help plan the four slides, simplify the ideas, and organize the presentation. I then checked an important fact and edited the final version so I could explain every slide myself.
Real-life useAI can help plan presentations, simplify complex ideas, suggest visuals, and improve organization—but the presenter still needs to verify information and make the final choices.
Presentation highlights

Slide 1: Why do NBA teams shoot so many 3s today?
Slide 2: A three-pointer rewards teams with an extra point from farther away.
Slide 3: Shooters pull defenders away from the basket and create space for drives and passes.
Slide 4: The 3-point shot changed scoring, spacing, and offensive strategy.

Fact-check from the assignment: the NBA introduced the 3-point line for the 1979–80 season.

What I learned

My biggest takeaway from the class is that using AI well is not only about generating something—it is about directing, checking, and improving it.

Give clear instructions

The quality of an AI result changes when the prompt is more specific about the goal, audience, style, and limits.

Check the output

AI can be useful without always being correct. Important facts, links, and claims should be verified before using them.

Make it my own

The first AI output is a starting point. My job is to edit it, choose what works, and make sure I understand the final product.
